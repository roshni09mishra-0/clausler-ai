4 Critical Traps Clausler Protects You Against

Over 78% of standard client MSAs contain one or more of these four fatal traps designed to shift enterprise liabilities onto individual contractors.
Trap #1: Catastrophic Financial Exposure Section 11.2 Typical

Unlimited Indemnification & Defense Duties

Clients frequently insert clauses forcing the contractor to "defend, indemnify, and hold harmless" from third-party lawsuits without limitation. On a $5,000 project, a frivolous software patent lawsuit can personally bankrupt you for hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Clausler Fix: Caps indemnification to fees paid under the agreement, restricts claims to direct willful misconduct, and deletes external defense costs.
Trap #2: Cashflow Strangulation Section 4.1 Typical

Net-90 Payment Terms & "Pay-When-Paid"

Many agreements delay payouts 90 days after deliverable acceptance, or declare payment conditional upon the client being paid by their own end-client. This forces the freelancer to act as an uncompensated bank.

Clausler Fix: Enforces standard Net-15 terms with automatic 1.5% monthly late interest and suspension rights for overdue invoices.
Trap #3: Overreaching IP Seizure Section 7.3 Typical

Perpetual IP & Pre-Existing Tool Grabs

Aggressive "work made for hire" definitions claim all inventions, scripts, and libraries created during the contract term—effectively transferring ownership of your personal open-source libraries or design frameworks to the client.

Clausler Fix: Explicitly reserves pre-existing tools, libraries, and background IP, transferring ownership only to customized deliverables upon receipt of full payment.
Trap #4: Career Handcuffs Section 9.4 Typical

Broad 2-Year Non-Compete Clauses

Clauses that restrict independent contractors from taking projects in similar business domains or with other industry clients for up to 24 months, severely limiting your future earning potential.

Clausler Fix: Replaces non-competes with non-solicitation of direct employees, ensuring you retain complete freedom to service the market.
